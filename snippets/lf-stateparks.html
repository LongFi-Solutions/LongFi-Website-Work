Parks went digital.
Indoor coverage did not follow.

Entry passes, camping reservations, and parking fees now run through a visitor’s phone. Inside many park buildings, that phone has little or no carrier coverage.

The entrance station

Passes and parking are paid by phone at the gate. Where cell phone signal is weak, the scan stalls and the queue behind it backs up.

The camp store counter

The highest frequency transaction in the park runs across a payment path that depends on a working cellular connection.

The nature center

Interpretive programming has moved to codes and applications that assume a visitor’s phone can reach the network.

Park agencies are describing this themselves. One state system, publishing guidance on scan based payment in February 2026, tells visitors to join a Wi‑Fi network before scanning the code.1 Another publishes a live list of the park buildings where it has installed Wi‑Fi, down to the coverage area inside each one.2 A third moved parking payment system wide to a mobile application.3 Federal America the Beautiful passes went fully digital on January 1, 2026.4

1 Oregon State Parks, published guidance on scan based payment, February 20, 2026.  2 Washington State Parks, “Wi‑Fi in the Parks,” accessed September 2026.  3 Massachusetts Department of Conservation and Recreation, mobile parking payment rollout, May 13, 2022.  4 U.S. Department of the Interior, digital pass announcement, effective January 1, 2026.

Where the coverage reaches,
and where it does not

LongFi Connect works indoors. The coverage reaches the park buildings visitors actually use: visitor centers, lodges and inns, restaurants, camp stores, and registration buildings.

Where a property already runs outdoor rated access points, coverage extends to the serviced areas those access points reach, such as a cabin porch, a lodge terrace, a picnic shelter, or a campground loop beside its registration building.

Coverage does not extend across open acreage, onto trails, or into backcountry. That boundary is confirmed at qualification so expectations match what gets delivered.

Use the Wi‑Fi your park already operates

If your park uses systems such as Meraki, Aruba, UniFi, Ruckus, Cisco, Juniper Mist, or other Passpoint-compatible Wi‑Fi, your buildings are likely already compatible.
